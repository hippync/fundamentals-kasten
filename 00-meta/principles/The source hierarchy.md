# The source hierarchy

*Principle · Part 5 · The Fundamentals Reset (Field Manual, Edition 2026)*

Always work top-down:

1. **Primary sources.** Specs, RFCs, official docs, the actual paper, the source code.
2. **Operators.** People who ran the thing at scale and wrote about what broke. Engineering blogs from companies that actually operate systems.
3. **Educators with a track record.** A small number of people who explain things correctly and have done so for years.
4. **Everything else.** Aggregators, hot takes, threads, newsletters, conference-talk summaries.

**Noise signals — discount immediately:**
- "X is dead" (X is never dead)
- Benchmark claims with no methodology
- Anything with a timeline for AGI
- Tutorials that are wrappers around a tool's own docs
- Advice about a tool from someone who has never run it in production

**Signal signals:**
- Postmortems and incident write-ups
- "Here's what we tried that didn't work"
- Anything with numbers and a described methodology
- Explanations of *why* a design was chosen over alternatives

**Curation policy:** keep a small, fixed set of tier-1/tier-2 sources. Check them on a schedule, not on a feed. The feed is designed to make you feel behind — that feeling is the product, not information.
