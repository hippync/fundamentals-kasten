# Sources — index

Curation policy, from Part 5: keep a small, fixed set of tier-1/tier-2 sources. Check them on a schedule, not on a feed. The feed is designed to make you feel behind — that feeling is the product, not information.

## The bookshelf, if you buy three

1. **[[Designing Data-Intensive Applications]]** — the most useful engineering book of the last fifteen years.
2. **[[Computer Systems — A Programmer's Perspective]]** — everything under your code.
3. **[[A Philosophy of Software Design]]** — short. Read it twice a year.

Then, in order: **[[Extreme Programming Explained]]** for how the work gets done, **[[The Pragmatic Programmer]]** for craft, and **[[Debugging (Agans)]]** for the skill that never gets automated.

## Free and excellent

- [[Operating Systems — Three Easy Pieces]] — Free online, genuinely readable.
- [[The Little Book of Semaphores]] — Free, exercise-driven.
- [[High Performance Browser Networking]] — Free online, excellent.
- [[Beej's Guide to Network Programming]] — Free, hands-on sockets.
- [[Use The Index, Luke!]] — Free, focused, immediately practical.
- [[Site Reliability Engineering]] — Free online.
- [[Pro Git]] — Free; read the "Git Internals" chapter — it's the one that makes everything click.
- [[Missing Semester of Your CS Education]] — Free lectures on exactly the things no course teaches.
- [[Neural Networks — Zero to Hero]] — Build a GPT from scratch — the manual calls this the single highest-value item on the AI list.

## Primary sources, papers, and operators

Part 5's hierarchy: primary sources first, then operators who ran it at scale, then educators with a track record.

- [[The Agile Manifesto]] *(primary)* — Four lines. Read the original, not the industry's version of it.
- [[Royce 1970 — Managing the Development of Large Software Systems]] *(paper)* — Described the sequential waterfall model and then argued it was risky and you should iterate. The industry kept the diagram and discarded the argument.
- [[Latency Numbers Every Programmer Should Know]] *(reference)* — Memorize the shape of it, not the digits.
- [[The Raft paper]] *(paper)* — Deliberately written to be understandable. Take the offer.
- [[Fallacies of Distributed Computing Explained]] *(paper)* — Short, essential.
- [[No Silver Bullet]] *(paper)* — 1986, still the best essay on why this is hard.
- [[OWASP Top 10 and Cheat Sheet Series]] *(primary)* — Free, practical. For each item: the mechanism and the mitigation.
- [[Attention Is All You Need]] *(paper)* — The primary source for the architecture.
- [[The Illustrated Transformer]] *(reference)* — The block-diagram-level explanation.
- [[The RAG paper]] *(paper)* — The original, for the concept. Then read honest engineering write-ups about retrieval failures, not vendor blog posts.
- [[Provider API documentation]] *(primary)* — The layer people skip and then get surprised by their bill. Read it properly.
- [[Provider prompting documentation]] *(primary)* — Skip the "1000 prompts" listicles entirely — they're the jQuery plugins of this era.
- [[Chip Huyen on AI engineering]] *(operator)* — This area has the worst signal-to-noise ratio of anything in AI — be picky.
- [[Hamel Husain on evals]] *(operator)* — Evals writing, from someone who ships them.
- [[Google engineering practices — code review]] *(primary)* — Free, short, unusually good on the communication side.
- [[Public postmortems]] *(operator)* — Any well-written public postmortem. Read them for the *writing*, not just the incident.
